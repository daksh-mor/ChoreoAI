### Potential Benefits and Drawbacks of AI in Creative Processes

A key question I’ve thought about is whether AI enhances or diminishes creativity. I had a moment of realization when listening to a song (Rather lie) that I initially loved, only to later find out it was AI-generated. This raised conflicting feelings—on one hand, I was impressed by the quality, but on the other, I felt slightly disconnected knowing it wasn’t purely human-made.

For dance, I believe AI should serve as a creative assistant rather than a replacement. If a dancer organically integrates AI-generated moves into their own choreography, it can be a valuable tool. However, fully AI-generated dance could lack the human emotion that makes performances so compelling. AI should enhance human creativity, not take over.

### Ideas for Multimodal Representations of Dance

I am still exploring possibilities, but one core idea is using **GNNs to create embeddings of dance movements**. Beyond this, some exciting directions we could explore include:

- **Graph-Based Dance Representation** – Treating dance sequences as graphs, where body joints are nodes and edges represent movement transitions. This could allow AI to model fluid and realistic motion patterns.
- **LLMs for Choreographic Description & Generation** – Fine-tuning an LLM to understand dance notation (like Labanotation) and generate step-by-step choreography based on music or textual prompts.
- **Multimodal AI for Dance & Music Synchronization** – Using AI to analyze beats, rhythm, and movement patterns to help choreographers align steps with music seamlessly.


I see this as not just an academic or research project but a potential step toward revolutionizing AI-assisted choreography.
